01Momentum conservation↗

formula
m₁u₁+m₂u₂ = m₁v₁+m₂v₂
true for EVERY collision — elastic or not
why always true
from Newton's 3rd law (F₁₂=−F₂₁), external force ≈0 during short collision
explosion from rest
m₁v₁ = m₂v₂
KE₁/KE₂ = m₂/m₁ (હલકાને વધુ KE)
યાદ
momentum સંરક્ષિત, KE નહીં (રાસાયણિક → KE)

02Collision types + restitution↗

coefficient of restitution
e = (v₂−v₁)/(u₁−u₂)
separation ÷ approach speed
range
e=1: elastic · 0<e<1: inelastic · e=0: perfectly inelastic
ElasticInelastic
momentumconservedconserved
KEconservednot conserved

03Perfectly elastic (1D)↗

general
v₁=[(m₁−m₂)u₁+2m₂u₂]/(m₁+m₂)
general
v₂=[(m₂−m₁)u₂+2m₁u₁]/(m₁+m₂)
special case
m₁=m₂, u₂=0 ⟹ v₁=0, v₂=u₁ (exchange — Newton's cradle)

04Perfectly inelastic (1D)↗

formula
v = (m₁u₁+m₂u₂)/(m₁+m₂)
objects stick, common velocity

05Energy loss↗

general
ΔKE = KE_before − KE_after
perfectly inelastic (u₂=0)
ΔKE = [m₁m₂/(m₁+m₂)]×½u₁²
where energy goes
heat, sound, permanent deformation — not destroyed

06Collision with wall↗

formula
v = e u
direction reversed; wall = infinite mass (not zero!)
elastic wall (e=1)
same speed, direction fully reversed

07Oblique / 2D collision — JEE extension↗

x-momentum
m₁u₁ = m₁v₁cosθ₁+m₂v₂cosθ₂
y-momentum
0 = m₁v₁sinθ₁−m₂v₂sinθ₂
special result
equal mass, one at rest, elastic ⟹ θ₁+θ₂=90°

!Common mistakes

Momentum conserves in EVERY collision; KE only in elastic
e formula: separation ÷ approach — order matters
Equal-mass velocity exchange only when one mass starts at rest
Wall = infinite mass, not zero mass
2D collision: x and y momentum conserved separately, never combined into one scalar equation
